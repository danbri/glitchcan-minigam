    Route on foot, above and below ground

    Or press and hold on the model: "route from here" and "route to here" pick the nearest mapped walkway or platform. Use the depth gauge on the left first to look below ground.

    Style

    Pixel art: an isometric view in a 33-colour palette with stylised trees, people, cyclists, buses, cabs, now and then a police car, ambulance or fire engine, gulls, river traffic (Thames Clipper catamarans, tourist boats, tugs towing waste barges, aggregate barges, speedboats) and swimmers in Eden Dock. Drag to move, pinch or wheel to zoom, the arrows at the top turn the view. The animals and traffic are illustrative, not live data.

    Gaussian splats

    Splats are drawn back to front and tested against the model's depth, so towers, glow outlines and routes stay in front of or behind them correctly. "Splats only" keeps the model in the depth buffer without drawing it.

    Music: the towers as a visualiser

    The synthesised splats are grouped by building; each building follows one of 24 frequency bands, west (bass) to east (treble). Play starts "Splats only" on the estate.

    Glow: who is inside

    Buildings glow when a source states that such an occupant is inside: OSM tags, Wikidata classes, the FSA business type or the Canary Wharf Group directory. Registered offices are not counted. Wikidata occupant links have no dates, so some are former tenants.

    Ground, building heights and open track are measured (EA LiDAR 1 m). The depth gauge on the left removes everything higher than the level you set, so you can look into the ground; the ground and water stay as faint context. Basement depths and indoor levels use OSM and Wikidata storey counts × the storey height setting. Tunnel levels are modelled between known levels: portals and open cuts the LiDAR can see, and published station levels (yellow labels, with sources).

    One finger: rotate. Two fingers: move and zoom. Tap: record. Press and hold: route from or to that point.

    Live data

    Nothing is fetched until you press the button. The data comes straight from each publisher's open API (no key); the full catalogue of checked sources is at feeds.
