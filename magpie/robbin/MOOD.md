# The mood we are after — notes from Gathering Sky's reception

UP THE JUNCTION is inspired by *Gathering Sky* (A Stately Frog, 2015):
a wordless, ~45-minute experience of guiding a flock of birds across
the sky, with an orchestral score performed by the San Francisco
Conservatory of Music. These notes distil what its reviewers said the
game FELT like, as a north star for our tube journey. (Summaries in
our own words; short phrases quoted from the cited reviews.)

## What reviewers felt

- **Meditation, not challenge.** "Calm; chill; zen" — it "takes the
  edge off of reality". "The least game-y game I've played" was meant
  as praise. It "feels like something akin to meditation".
- **No punishment, ever.** "Nothing in the game exists to punish the
  user; everything enhances the idea of exploration." Collisions
  merely "disrupt the flock" for a moment. You cannot lose; you can
  only continue.
- **The flock is the protagonist.** Birds "wheel and regroup in
  mid-air"; the story is companionship — starting alone, gathering
  others, struggling to stay together. Togetherness IS the reward.
- **The score carries the emotion.** Music "sets the tone of each
  level", staying serene in calm passages and "erupting with
  triumphant stirring strings" at speed; storms get "more frantic
  melodies" under "an over-arcing theme". Adaptive audio was called
  its strongest selling point.
- **Variety of weather, not of difficulty.** "Some levels were
  peaceful and playful, while others managed to whip up the tension"
  — tension as *weather*, never as fail state.
- **Sparse on purpose.** "Refreshingly bereft of minutiae" — no
  scores, no trophies, no clutter; what few discoveries exist feel
  precious.
- **An ending that lands.** The finale "left my chest a little tight
  and my eyes a little damp… something as simple as the right note or
  the right image can strike your heart."
- **Honest brevity.** Short, and better for it; "finishing in an hour
  seems like a poor choice" — dawdling is the intended pace.

## How UP THE JUNCTION lines up

Already aligned: no clock, no lives, no fail state; brushing a
commuter is a flutter, not a death; the flock swirls boids-fashion
and grows with every rescue; the music assembles layer by layer as
the flock does (`Chiptune.setIntensity`), swelling at reunions; quest
cards read like little stories; even the glitch-grab is indignant
comedy, not punishment.

Standing tuning principles, from the list above:

1. **Never add a fail state to Up the Junction.** Weather, yes;
   losing, no.
2. **Emotion belongs in the music first.** New moments should get an
   audio gesture before they get UI.
3. **Keep the surface sparse.** Prefer removing a label to adding
   one. Discoveries (buried wonders, the Konami roll) stay unlabelled
   and precious.
4. **The flock is the protagonist** — screens should keep filling
   with bird buddies, and moments of *regrouping* (gather beats,
   murmurations) deserve as much care as moments of progress.
5. **Make the ending land.** Completing the flock is the emotional
   peak — it gets a murmuration, the full band, and time to breathe
   (see the finale in `robbin-tube.js`).
