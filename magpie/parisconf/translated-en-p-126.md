## Title
SOME NEW ANALOGIES BETWEEN COMPUTING MACHINES STRUCTURES AND BRAIN STRUCTURES

## Content

The arithmetical machines have a permanent memory and a temporary one. Their proper working is made of short pulses switched through complex circuits.

The structure analogy of a synapse and a transistor leads to assume the existence of synaptic flip-flops, which are permanent memory elements, and of an electronic impregnation of synaptic texture, which is a phenomenon of temporary storage.

Pascal's rule of logical research: substitution the definite object by its definition, suggests a mean for edifying concepts which leads to locate the rules of the logic in the structure of nervous circuits; so, some agnosias are easily explicated. But it must be admitted, for anatomical reasons, that the brain contains a permanent memory and a temporary one. Their working, such as it must be assumed to explain several psychopathological phenomena, leads to a biological definition of the psychological conscience and of the sub-conscious.

A work of Mr Belin-Milleron was implies to admit that every man's reasoning is normally based on a few steady ideas, the system of which characterises the civilization to which that man belongs. The consideration of the perturbations which this steady ideas system may undergo, together with the permanent and the temporary memory, brings some precisions about the notions of: genius, paranoiac and paranoid states, autistic memory, sensorio-motive memory.

## Translation Notes

[Original in English] - This abstract was written in English by Louis Couffignal.

[Translation note: Louis Couffignal (1902-1966) was a French computing pioneer who directed the Institut Blaise Pascal and organized this conference. His work sought to bridge mechanical computation and neuroscience.]

[Translation note: The comparison of synapses to transistors was cutting-edge in 1951 - the transistor had only been invented in 1947, and its application to computing was just beginning. Couffignal's "synaptic flip-flops" anticipates decades of neural network research.]

[Translation note: "Pascal's rule of logical research" refers to Blaise Pascal's philosophical method of defining terms precisely before reasoning - appropriate given the conference was held at the Institut Blaise Pascal.]

[Translation note: "Agnosias" - Neurological conditions involving loss of ability to recognize objects, persons, sounds, or other stimuli. Couffignal suggests these can be explained by circuit-like logic failures in the brain.]

[Translation note: The distinction between "permanent memory" and "temporary memory" in both machines and brains anticipates the later distinction between long-term and short-term (working) memory in cognitive science.]

[Translation note: "Belin-Milleron" - Collaborator of Couffignal whose work on cultural determinants of reasoning is referenced.]

[Translation note: "Sensorio-motive memory" - An early term for what would later be called procedural memory or motor memory.]

[Original typos/gallicisms: "permanente" → "permanent"; "somme" → "some"; "caracterises" → "characterises" - corrected in translation]
