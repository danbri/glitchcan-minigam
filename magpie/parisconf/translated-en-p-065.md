# Page 65

## Translation from French

Dr. Paul CHAUCHARD

THE CENTRAL COMMAND OF THE NERVOUS MACHINE

It is customary to consider in the nervous machine primarily the elementary aspect: the properties of nervous impulses and their passage through an organized neuronal structure comprising numerous self-regulating reactive circuits would suffice to explain the harmony of the machine's functioning. In fact, as Lapicque indicates, this is sufficient only at the level of the simplest reflex; to understand the integral complexity of nervous functioning, notably in the cerebral cortex, one must invoke a central regulatory station that centralizes all self-regulation messages and keeps all neurons under its control.

The existence of such a centre analogous to the programming organ of machines and which organizes the nervous system in advance for the action to be accomplished is proven by two distinct orders of experimental considerations: on one hand, the demonstration of a regulatory mechanism for wakefulness, sleep and mood in the base of the encephalon, and on the other hand, the localization in this same region through chronaximetry of a centre of subordination that keeps all neurons under its control regarding their state of dynamogenesis or inhibition and regulates them according to needs. These two mechanisms can be identified, with sleep appearing as a resting state of the subordination centre.

The analogy between nervous execution mechanisms and machines is remarkable; both serve thought. But it would be an error to draw from this an argument for a dualistic conception of thought, returning to that of Descartes who, in a region near the base of the brain, wished to see the point of insertion of the soul; however complex the psyche may be, it does not appear as a spirit acting incomprehensibly upon matter, but as the interior aspect, the emergence arising from the complexity of cerebral physiological functioning. Here the brain surpasses the machine, however complex it may be, for the emergence of a true reflective consciousness presupposes a true sensitivity that is the prerogative of life, which allows the nervous machine, even more self-regulated than the artificial machine, to construct itself. Modern psycho-physiology, surpassing the sterile oppositions between materialism and spiritualism, considers the living being as a unity.

## Translation Notes

- "machine nerveuse" translated as "nervous machine" - period terminology for the nervous system viewed through a mechanistic lens
- "chronaximétrie" - chronaximetry, a technique for measuring nerve excitability developed by Lapicque
- "dynamogénie" - dynamogenesis, an increase in nervous activity/excitability
- [Translation note: "psycho-physiologie" anticipates what would later be called "cognitive neuroscience" - a field that did not exist in 1951]
- The author explicitly rejects Cartesian dualism while maintaining that consciousness "emerges" from physiological complexity - a position that prefigures later debates in philosophy of mind
