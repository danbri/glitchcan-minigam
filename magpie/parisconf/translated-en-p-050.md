One of the hypotheses that simple observation of cerebral structures can suggest, when, in their disconcerting monomorphism, one compares them to calculating machines, is that the distribution of information must be done there, as in these, with the aid of multiple-position switching devices. This hypothesis has already been implied by McCulloch and Walter. The Author believes he has brought to the hypothesis of a diencephalic "switching" of afferent information some physiological proofs, the most valid of which are: a) the experiments he made in Montreal with John Hunter where he shows that the cerebral response to light, at first isolated in the retino-geniculo-occipital system alone, is suddenly "oriented" toward the thalamus and frontal cortex when one injects a sufficient quantity of cardiazol; b) the findings he has made in numerous epileptics where the response to light can occupy the occipital cortex as well as the frontal, temporal or parietal. Such findings seem indeed to invalidate the classical conclusions and leave one to presume on the contrary that there exist at the level of the metathalamus, "possibilities" of junctions between these pathways and other thalamofugal pathways. This notion provides a simple physiological substratum for the phenomenon of sensory interpenetration. It explains this harmonious dispersion of information without which psychic life could not present that dynamic and proteomorphic aspect, haloed and fringed, that William James has accustomed us to recognize in it.

At the moment of concluding, must one answer the question: can calculating machines think? The Author believes that calculating machines have been made by men and for them; they were made only to accomplish quickly and well long and tedious operations; they have moreover the unforeseen advantage of being an extremely useful model for whoever would wish to compare to them the associative regions of a brain. They provide from this point of view, to the psycho-physiologist, an important occasion for "thinking", but it would be at the least premature to suppose that they themselves might be capable of doing so.

- [Translation note: The reference to William James's conception of consciousness as "haloed and fringed" comes from his "stream of consciousness" psychology. The concept of "harmonious dispersion of information" anticipates distributed processing in neural networks.]
