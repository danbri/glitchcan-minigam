The future calculating machines in the NBS programme are binary
machines, with the exception of those from Eckert-Mauchly which are
decimal-coded machines. The high-speed memory components, commonly
called internal memory components, include: magnetic drum, acoustic
delay line, and electrostatic memory. Furthermore, among these
calculating machines, there are machines that operate in "serial" mode,
others in "parallel" mode, or "serial-parallel" combinations. Among
the input and output means, there are perforated paper tape, magnetic
tape, and magnetic wire. The base pulse repetition rate varies among
machines over a range of approximately two megacycles. The extent of
internal error-detection circuits varies from none to nearly complete
systems. Because of the different variable parameters in the plans
for the seven calculators, it is hoped that experience from their use
will provide valuable information for determining the best way to plan
future machines.

The first calculator put into service in the NBS calculating
machine programme was the SEAC. The SWAC is still in limited use;
the others are in the process of completion. Therefore, the
construction characteristics and operational experience with the SEAC
(mainly), and the SWAC, are treated here in greater detail.

## Notes
- INA = Institute for Numerical Analysis (NBS facility in Los Angeles, hosted at UCLA)
- The comparison of binary vs. decimal machines was an active design question
- Three memory technologies are mentioned: magnetic drum, mercury delay lines (acoustic), and Williams tubes (electrostatic)
- Serial vs. parallel architecture was another fundamental design choice
- Two megacycles = 2 MHz pulse rate, fast for 1950
- [Translation note: "circuit a retard acoustique" = acoustic delay line, typically mercury-filled tubes used for memory]
