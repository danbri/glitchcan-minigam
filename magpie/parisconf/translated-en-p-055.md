- 5 -

by my father calculate the value of a polynomial with the different powers of x. When one gives x all values in a wide range, one must observe if the value of the polynomial passes through zero: the value of x at that moment is one of the roots of the equation obtained by setting the value of the polynomial equal to zero.

All values are written on drums in logarithmic scales. Thus to obtain a product it suffices to add the values of the angular displacements of the 2 drums, which is easy by means of an epicyclic gear train. A power is easily obtained by multiplying the angular displacement by the exponent; one arrives at this by means of a fixed gear train, but an addition is more difficult; and to have the value of the polynomial one must necessarily make the addition of the monomials. This is what my father obtained by constructing mechanically the system of Gaussian additive logarithms. The endless screw expresses the relation
y = log (10^x + 1).

The curve represented by this formula has two asymptotes; one substitutes the curve by asymptotes from the points where it approaches them sufficiently. One of the asymptotes is horizontal, which corresponds to an infinite ratio of speeds. This is obtained by means of a device composed of several gears. The central part of the curve, which is not substituted by asymptotes, corresponds to a variable ratio of speeds, which is obtained by a special gear.

## Notes
- [Translation note: The "endless screw" (fusee sans fin) was Torres-Quevedo's ingenious mechanical solution for computing addition in logarithmic space - a fundamental challenge for analog computing. Gaussian additive logarithms allow addition to be performed mechanically when numbers are represented as logarithms, enabling the polynomial evaluation needed for equation solving.]
