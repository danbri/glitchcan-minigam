5. Negative tropism. When the model encounters a material obstacle, a circuit is formed to transform the internal amplifier into a multivibrator, leading to alternating movements of coming and going combined with a change of direction, so that it displaces the obstacle, passes over it or goes around it. It "remembers" the obstacle for about one second.

6. Discernment. When the behaviour of the model is of mode 5, all other modes are impossible, so that the model is indifferent to positive and distant excitations while it remains accessible to immediate negative excitations.

7. Internal homeostasis. When the internal motive power is about to be exhausted, mode 3 becomes impossible, so that the model can approach very close to a light source and, if it can use suitable connections, it will recover its motive power; during this operation, all other modes are impossible.

8. Self-recognition. A small pilot light source is connected to the circuit of the searching and direction servo-motor. If the model receives a signal from its own light source reflected in a mirror, this source is extinguished. The signal is thus suppressed and the searching and direction servo-motor is again connected, but this reactivates the signal, and so forth. Oscillations occur which engender a specific pattern of behaviour.

9. Mutual recognition. Let there be two such models, each receiving signals from the pilot lamp of the other; each will extinguish its own, and again complex oscillations will be established between the two models, engendering a characteristic pattern of "social" behaviour.


LEARNING BY ASSOCIATION.- The addition of two or three supplementary tubes confers on the model the power to establish and suppress simple conditioned reflexes. The minimum conditions required can be stated as follows:

1. Differentiation, as to the duration of specific signals
2. Extension, as to the duration of neutral signals
3. Mixtures of specific and neutral excitations in coincidence
4. Summation at the moment of coincidences

## Notes
- [Translation note: Walter's description of "self-recognition" behavior - the robot oscillating when seeing its own light in a mirror - is remarkably prescient, anticipating later discussions of machine self-awareness. The description of "social behavior" between two robots and of "neuroses" (mentioned on next page) shows how cybernetic thinking was already using psychological terminology to describe machine behavior.]
