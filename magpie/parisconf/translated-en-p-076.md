Figure 4: There exists in the lower part of the medulla a reticular formation; the largest cells composing it send long axons descending in the ventromedial tract of the spinal cord that will stimulate interneurons whose axons ascend and descend in the ventral spinal funiculus to regain the grey matter and ramify there in fine arborizations.

Figure 5 shows how they approach the soma of the motoneuron, and according to CAJAL it is thought that these arborizations terminate principally at the level of the dendritic tree of the motoneuron. Impulses that come to die in this region present a "sink" along the dendrites and a "source" near the soma of the motoneurons. Due to the general organization of this system descending from the medulla, its action is best observed in the case of repetitive discharges. A polarization of the motoneuron occurs that raises its threshold.

Figure 6 shows the electrotonic image in the anterior spinal root of this polarization in the form of a positive deflection (Cf. upper part of the figure, positivities being recorded downward). At the same time a negative electrotonic deflection is recorded from the dorsal roots. The elevation of the threshold of the motoneuron soma can be demonstrated by the use of antidromic stimulations.

Figure 7 shows that furthermore the inhibitory impulses of this system, which by virtue of these anatomical arrangements come to fail in the region where the excitatory arborizations from the posterior root terminate, polarize these excitatory terminals and thus prevent the development of a normal presynaptic potential. The trace in the center of the figure demonstrates such an inhibition. The presynaptic potential is diminished and the motoneuron's response is absent.

- "formation reticulée" - reticular formation, a network of neurons in the brainstem crucial for arousal and consciousness
- "electrotonic" - referring to passive electrical spread in neural tissue
- "antidromique" - antidromic, propagation of impulses in the opposite direction to normal (toward the cell body)
