DEVELOPMENT OF ELECTRONIC ARITHMETIC CALCULATING MACHINES

AT THE NATIONAL BUREAU OF STANDARDS

A brief history of the origins of the National Bureau of Standards'
calculating machine development programme will be given.
This programme encompasses four main phases of activity: (1) fundamental
research, (2) developments pertaining to the engineering art,
(3) machine design and construction, and (4) technical services.
The Bureau's objectives for these four phases of activity
in the calculator field will be briefly outlined.

Technical services for the United States Government constitute
perhaps the most important part of the NBS calculating machine
programme. In addition to consulting services for Federal Agencies,
the Bureau acts as technical coordinator for contracts with industry
for five calculating machines:

a) Three machines for the following agencies: Bureau of the
Census, Air Comptroller Service (accounting) and Army
Map Service. These machines are under construction
at the Eckert-Mauchly Computer Corporation.

b) One machine for the Office of Air Research, under construction
at the General Electric Company.

c) One machine sponsored by the Office of Naval Research, and
used by the NBS Computing Laboratory, under construction at the
Raytheon Manufacturing Company.

Furthermore, the Bureau has constructed two calculating machines, the
SEAC (National Bureau of Standards Eastern Automatic Computer) and the
SWAC (National Bureau of Standards Western Automatic Computer), sponsored
by the Air Comptroller of the Air Ministry, the Army Ministry,
and the Bureau.

## Notes
- NBS = National Bureau of Standards (now NIST)
- SEAC was completed in 1950, one of the first stored-program computers in the US
- SWAC was completed in 1950 at the NBS Institute for Numerical Analysis in Los Angeles
- Eckert-Mauchly built UNIVAC I, delivered to the Census Bureau in 1951
- The four-phase programme structure reflects the post-war institutionalization of computing
- [Translation note: "machines a calculer arithmetiques electroniques" = electronic arithmetic calculating machines - distinguishing digital from analog machines]
