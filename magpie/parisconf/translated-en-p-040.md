Dr. A. van WIJNGAARDEN


ROUNDING ERRORS IN SYSTEMATIC CALCULATIONS


The effects, more or less serious, that rounding errors can have in calculations are well known, and several efforts have been made to evaluate their influence, for example in the numerical solution of differential equations. These efforts are extremely valuable, but often rounding errors have been treated entirely as statistical quantities, whereas they are in reality quantities entirely defined from the mathematical point of view. Although, in the majority of cases, it is permissible to consider them as statistical quantities, there are other cases in which a more rigorous study must be made. The purpose of the lecture is to illustrate this fact by some examples.

Systematic calculations can be of different types. On one hand, a certain operation can be performed numerous times on variables, in such a way that the result of one operation does not enter into the following operation. This case presents itself for example in systematic interpolation (or the formation of a denser table) for a given table of values of an already existing function, or one of the other linear operations such as numerical differentiation or integration in a small domain. At the opposite extreme, the second case presents itself in iteration procedures where the operands are the very results of the last operation; for example, let us cite the solutions of homogeneous recurrence relations and of differential equations. Between these two extreme cases, we find iteration methods in which foreign data are constantly introduced, for example the solutions of non-homogeneous recurrence relations and of differential equations, as well as integration in a large domain. Some simple cases serve as illustrations, in part to show the very curious phenomenon that can present itself if one considers the exact theory
