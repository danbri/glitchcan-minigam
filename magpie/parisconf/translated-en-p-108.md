## Notes
[Handwritten annotation in top right corner: "10" with a curved line/bracket]
[Handwritten annotation: "engl." appears to indicate this section's papers are in English]

[Translation note: "Les Machines a Calculer et la Pensee Humaine" - This is the full title of the conference. "Calculating Machines" was the standard term before "computer" became common (the French word "ordinateur" would not be coined until 1955). "Human Thought" rather than "cognition" reflects the pre-cognitive-science era.]

[Translation note: "Les Grosses Machines" - literally "the large machines," referring to mainframe-scale computing equipment. This was before miniaturization made smaller computers possible.]

[Translation note: Section 3 addresses the intersection of computing, logic, and brain physiology - the intellectual territory that would later become artificial intelligence and cognitive science, but here discussed without those unified frameworks.]
