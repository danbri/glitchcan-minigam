Dr. E. STIEFEL

THE ARITHMETIC CALCULATING MACHINE "Z4"

OF THE SWISS FEDERAL INSTITUTE OF TECHNOLOGY AT ZURICH

and its application in the resolution of a partial differential equation

The calculating machine "Z4" was commissioned by the Institute of
Applied Mathematics of the Swiss Federal Institute of Technology,
and constructed by Konrad Zuse, taking into account the instructions and
suggestions of the Institute.

Brief technical description: Precision, 6 decimal digits;
internal representation in the binary numeration system - Floating
binary point - Arithmetic unit and control circuits operating
with relays - The memory contains original and purely
mechanical compartments invented by Zuse. (The memory capacity is currently
only 64 numbers). The machine contains 2,200 relays and 21
switches. Time required for one multiplication: 2.5 sec. The
commands are given by perforated tape (35 mm cinema film) and are
fed by means of two reading stations that can be used alternately.
Results are given by lamps, or by a printer, or by perforated paper.
Operations included in the machine comprise division and square root.
Some particular properties will also be described.

The procedure for extracting roots and for calculations involving
"particular values" 0, infinity, and ? presents arithmetical interest.
From the organizational point of view, it is interesting to note that the
tape containing the sequence of operations is prepared in a convenient manner,
presenting some analogies with Aiken's Mark III machine. There are in total
50 command keys as well as 64 keys for the 64 memory positions.

## Notes
- Konrad Zuse built the world's first programmable computers (Z1-Z4) in Germany
- The Z4 was the only Zuse machine to survive WWII and was leased to ETH Zurich
- "Floating binary point" = floating-point arithmetic, advanced for its time
- The use of 35mm cinema film as tape storage was an ingenious adaptation
- 2.5 seconds per multiplication was typical for relay-based machines
- The "particular values" handling (0, infinity, undefined) anticipates IEEE floating-point standards
- [Translation note: "point binaire flottant" is translated as "floating binary point" - the term "floating-point" was still evolving]
