## Translation from French

Louis COUFFIGNAL

SOME NEW ANALOGIES BETWEEN

CALCULATING MACHINE STRUCTURES AND CEREBRAL STRUCTURES

Arithmetic machines possess a permanent memory and a temporary memory. Their functioning is accomplished through brief impulses routed through complex circuits.

The structural analogy between a synapse and a transistor leads us to postulate the existence of synaptic flip-flops, organs of permanent memory, and an electronic impregnation of the synaptic tissue, a phenomenon of temporary memory.

Pascal's rule of logical inquiry: to replace the defined by the definition, suggests a mode of concept formation that leads to situating the rules of logic in the structure of nervous circuits; certain agnosias can thus be readily explained. But for anatomical reasons, one must admit that the brain contains a permanent memory and a temporary memory. Their functioning, as one must conceive it to explain certain psychopathological phenomena, leads to a biological definition of psychological consciousness and the subconscious.

Work by Ms Belin-Milleron would suggest that all men normally base their reasoning on a single system of fixed ideas, few in number, which characterizes the civilization to which they belong. Consideration of the perturbations that this system of fixed ideas can undergo, as well as the organs of permanent and temporary memory, brings certain clarifications to the notions of: genius, paranoid and paranoiac states, autistic memory, sensory-motor memory.

## Translation Notes

- "machine à calculer" / "machines arithmétiques" translated as "calculating machine" / "arithmetic machines" - period terminology before "computer" became standard
- "flip-flops synaptiques" - Couffignal is proposing that synapses might function like electronic flip-flop circuits (bistable multivibrators used for memory in early calculating machines)
- [Translation note: The transistor had only been invented in 1947; Couffignal's comparison of synapses to transistors was remarkably prescient]
- "mémoire au tistique" appears to be "mémoire autistique" (autistic memory) - possibly referring to what would later be called eidetic or savant-like memory
- [Translation note: Couffignal's distinction between "permanent memory" and "temporary memory" anticipates the later distinction between long-term and short-term memory in cognitive psychology]
