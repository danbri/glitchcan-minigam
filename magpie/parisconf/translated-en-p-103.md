## Content

M. V. WILKES


OPERATIONAL EXPERIENCE OF THE E.D.S.A.C.


The paper will contain an account of the experience
which has been obtained in the use of the E.D.S.A.C. for solving mathematical problems since it first began to work in the
summer of 1949.

There will be particular reference to the way in
which orders are punched on the tape and to the library of subroutine. Some remarks will be made about the organisation necessary to make a machine serve concurrently the interests of a number of different users.

---

## Notes
[Original in English]

Maurice V. Wilkes was director of the Cambridge University Mathematical Laboratory and principal designer of EDSAC (Electronic Delay Storage Automatic Calculator), which became operational on May 6, 1949 - one of the first practical stored-program electronic computers.

[Translation note: "library of subroutine" - Wilkes was a pioneer in developing the concept of subroutine libraries, later formalized in his 1951 book with Wheeler and Gill, "The Preparation of Programs for an Electronic Digital Computer."]

[Translation note: "orders" - the period term for what would later be called "instructions" in computer programming]
