This memoir was published by the Academy in the collection of foreign scholars. Among the constructed devices which are attached to the principles expounded in this memoir, there are two algebraic machines which give the real roots of equations up to the 9th degree; a machine for solving a 2nd degree equation with complex coefficients and a machine which gives all the particular solutions one wants of given differential equations.

Shortly after, he became preoccupied with remote control devices. In 1903, he presented to the French Academy an apparatus which he had already had constructed, which he named "telekine", which could serve to control remotely a boat or a dirigible balloon. Trials were made in the following years in Madrid and Bilbao, in the presence of the King and a considerable crowd. A boat commanded from the shore accomplished perfectly the desired manoeuvres. Before the telekine, experiments in remote control had been made but for much simpler things, such as for example lighting and extinguishing a lamp. Shortly after the indicated date, various devices analogous to the telekine were tried: "on various occasions inventors no doubt in good faith believed themselves to be the first to use Hertzian waves to effect remote controls." The citation is from an article by M. Maurice d'OCAGNE, published in 1938. These works on remote control were, I believe, the beginning of his ideas on automatics, which he later developed on a larger scale.

In the month of January 1914, he published in the journal of the Academy of Sciences of Madrid a work which had as its title: "Essay on Automatics. Its definition. Theoretical extent of its applications". He showed "that it is always possible to construct an automaton all of whose acts depend on certain circumstances, more or less numerous, following rules that one can impose arbitrarily at the moment of construction."
