# Concept Index - Paris Colloquium 1951

## Calculating Machines and Human Thought
*International Colloquium, Institut Blaise Pascal, CNRS*
*Paris, January 8-13, 1951*

---

## Technical Concepts

### Calculating machines (machines a calculer)
Automatic devices for numerical and symbolic computation.
- **Pages**: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 17-20, 33-40, 65-68, 81-84, 97-100, 113-116
- **Note**: The French word "ordinateur" (computer) would not be coined until 1955

### Arithmetic machine (machine arithmetique)
Machine operating on numbers in discrete representation (as opposed to analog machines).
- **Pages**: 3, 17-20, 33-36
- **See also**: calculating machine

### Analog machine (machine analogique)
Machine using continuous physical quantities (voltages, currents) to represent mathematical variables.
- **Pages**: 3, 4, 37-44
- **See also**: differential analyzer

### Memory (memoire)
System for storing data and instructions in a machine.
- **Pages**: 17-20, 33-36, 65-68, 81-84, 97-98
- **Related terms**: magnetic drum, Williams tubes

### Magnetic drum (tambour magnetique)
Storage device using a metallic cylinder coated with magnetic material.
- **Pages**: 17-18, 33-36, 81-84

### Vacuum tubes (tubes)
Electronic components used as logic elements in early machines.
- **Pages**: 17-20, 33-36, 81-84
- **Note**: Tubes were the dominant technology before transistors

### Encoding (codage)
Representation of information in numerical or symbolic form.
- **Pages**: 17-20, 65-70

### Rounding-off errors (erreurs d'arrondi)
Errors introduced by finite representation of numbers in calculations.
- **Pages**: 100-104
- **See also**: van Wijngaarden

## Cybernetic Concepts

### Cybernetics (cybernetique)
Science of control and communication in animal and machine (Wiener's term, 1948).
- **Pages**: 5-8, 49-52, 105-108

### Human thought (la pensee humaine)
Central question of the colloquium: can machines imitate or reproduce thought?
- **Pages**: 1, 2, 5-8, 49-56, 105-112

### Brain (cerveau)
The biological organ of thought, compared to calculating machines.
- **Pages**: 5-8, 49-56, 105-112
- **Related terms**: associative regions, neurophysiology

### Feedback (retroaction)
Mechanism by which a system's output influences its input.
- **Pages**: 37-40, 49-52
- **See also**: servo-mechanisms

### Servo-mechanisms (servo-mecanismes)
Automatic control systems using feedback.
- **Pages**: 37-44

### Differential equations (equations differentielles)
Numerical solution of differential equations on machines.

## Machines Mentioned

### Harvard Mark II, III, IV
Electromechanical and electronic calculators developed at Harvard under Aiken's direction.
- **Pages**: 1, 3, 81

### S.E.C. (Simple Electronic Computer)
Prototype built at Birkbeck, 250 tubes, 256 words of 6 decimals.
- **Pages**: 81-84

### A.P.E.X.C. (All Purpose Electronic X-ray Computer)
Final Birkbeck machine, 275 tubes, 512 words of 9 decimals.
- **Pages**: 81-84

### A.C.E. (Automatic Computing Engine)
British computer from NPL, original design by Turing.
- **Pages**: 1, 3

### Zuse Machine
Calculator developed by Konrad Zuse, installed at ETH Zurich.
- **Pages**: 1, 3

## Historical Context

This colloquium takes place at a pivotal moment in computing history:

- **1943**: McCulloch-Pitts paper on formal neurons
- **1945**: von Neumann's EDVAC report
- **1948**: Wiener's "Cybernetics", Shannon's information theory
- **1950**: Turing's "Computing Machinery and Intelligence"
- **1951**: This colloquium
- **1955**: Invention of the French word "ordinateur" (computer) by Jacques Perret
- **1956**: Dartmouth Conference - birth of Artificial Intelligence

The term "calculating machines" is period-appropriate: the French word "ordinateur" (computer) would not be coined until 1955. Participants are debating questions that would become central to artificial intelligence and cognitive science, but these fields do not yet formally exist.

## Translation Notes

When reading these proceedings, note that:

1. **"Machine a calculer"** is the standard 1951 term for what we now call a "computer"
2. **"La pensee humaine"** (human thought) anticipates what would become "cognition" in cognitive science
3. **"Cybernetique"** was the dominant unifying framework - not yet "computer science" or "AI"
4. The brain/computer analogy was new and exciting, not yet a cliche
5. These were serious scientists asking genuinely open questions about the nature of mind and machine
