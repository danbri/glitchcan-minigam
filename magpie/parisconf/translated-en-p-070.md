## Translation from French

- 3 -

Finally, while existing calculating machines are capable of solving most problems that we have posed for them, none, to my knowledge, could understand the meaning of particles establishing logical connections if these are presented to it simply in the form of a collection of "pots" and "titties" called "bits" buried among other "pots and titties", a performance quickly learned by every child. Because of this, these machines are incapable of the kind of intuitive understanding that gives rise to mathematical theorems and original scientific hypotheses and that suddenly made us doubt what we had previously accepted with closed eyes. It may happen that we will come to build machines that will store indifferently operations and operators as so many "pots" and "titties" and that will interpret any collection of operations and operators as orders or numbers according to the result of previous operations. The ambiguity of accumulated data is necessary for an interchangeability of the categories of operations and operators, but it does not suffice. And I do not think we already know how to build or design machines that will be skilled enough to handle this mutation from one kind to another, without which no machine can have the idea of an idea. Thus defined, consciousness is at the heart of human thought, whether it uses "natural" or "conventional" terms.

From here on I shall speak only of thinking in natural terms, because we experiment on animals and not on men. And in this domain there are many resemblances between brains and calculating machines, particularly those of the digital type.

The difference between "digital" or "logical" machines and analog machines resides principally in the fact that for logical machines we fully use for each component an equilibrium state of the "all or nothing" type; by contrast, in analog machines we handle only certain statistical properties of the components but make no use of the particular states in which each component might find itself. We distinguish these statistical elements only by barely discernible differences. This is never precise but the uncertain quantity is known with the assurance that derives from the law of large numbers. If we had at our disposal enough relays, we could theoretically obtain the same precision with imprecise decisions, by calculating everything in parallel and averaging the results. This type of maneuver is at the brain's disposal and it makes use of it principally in the circuits where it records information concerning dynamic variables of the external world and by means of which it commands the muscular movements causing displacements of weight. In this case the brain resembles an analog machine and its relays are excited so as to determine how many of them in a given set respond rather than to determine which of them responds.

## Translation Notes

- "pots and titties" - McCulloch's playful terminology for binary states (0s and 1s, "bits")
- [Translation note: McCulloch's claim that machines cannot "have the idea of an idea" - i.e., engage in meta-cognition or self-reflection - remains a topic of debate in AI philosophy]
- "all or nothing" - referring to the all-or-none principle of neural firing discovered in the early 20th century
- [Translation note: The distinction between digital and analog computing was a major topic of the era; McCulloch argues the brain uses both principles]
