A COMPARISON BETWEEN CALCULATING MACHINES AND THE BRAIN

- I -

The way you have brought together in a single title "Calculating Machines" and human thought disturbs me. Brains are calculating machines but calculating machines made by man are not yet brains. The role of a nervous system is to control an organism in such a way that it and the species it represents survive in a world of constantly varying physical forces. This control is achieved through "negative feedbacks". Some of these, constituting closed circuits within the brain itself, maintain its own activity at a determined level, in a manner very similar to the automatic gain control of a radio receiver which maintains the amplitude sensibly constant across various reception ranges. Other closed circuits, having some of their elements in more peripheral parts of our body, maintain stable the central temperature, arterial pressure, blood pH, just as they will achieve the tension of our muscles necessary for a normal posture in a changing world. Thus, each of these circuits serves only to bring the variable, whose deviation is recorded by specific receptors, back to a pre-established value in relation to its counter-reaction. This is what Claude Bernard called the "maintenance of the constancy of the internal environment," CANNON homeostasis, realized by the "reflexes" described by MAGENDIE and BELL. Finally, "appetitive" circuits that "inverse over their targets" put us in possession of things we must procure from the world around us. If the variable that controls this circuit at each instant depends on the distance separating us from the goal to be reached, it is evidently "teleological" in the sense of WIENER, ROSENBLUETH and BIGELOW.
