3. The principal fields of use of automatic calculation are ballistics, nuclear physics, and aeronautics. Of these disciplines, only the last pertains to the Art of the Engineer. The applications of automatic calculation there are, already at present, considerable (United States). They concern calculations of structures, vibrations, etc.

It is now necessary to promote the use of machines in the domains of mechanics, electricity, civil engineering, industrial physics, etc. Applications there are still sporadic, but one perceives there large possibilities for expansion.

4. The problems for which methods must be developed immediately and in a systematic manner are:

a) The solution of systems of linear algebraic equations with a large number of unknowns;

b) The solution of ordinary differential equations;

c) The solution of partial differential equations.

Then comes harmonic analysis, the construction of numerical tables, etc.

5. It seems to us important to draw special attention to the following points:

a) Lack of comprehensive doctrines in all branches of automatic calculation;

b) Importance of the choice of machines (adaptation of equipment);

c) Absolute necessity of developing numerical analysis prior to any mechanization of scientific calculation;

d) Necessity of implementing calculation procedures appropriate to the mode of operation of the machines used (adaptation of methods).

6. We shall finally consider the problem of the creation and operation of calculation bureaus. The aspect of this problem is threefold: localization and statement of problems (engineer), elaboration of methods and formulation of calculations (mathematician), operation of machines (technician). These activities must be closely coordinated. They imply teamwork and a strong specialization of execution personnel.

The creation of central calculation bureaus cannot be avoided (economic point of view) by small countries nor by small and medium-sized industries.
