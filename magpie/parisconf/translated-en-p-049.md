Henri GASTAUT


CALCULATING MACHINES AND THE HUMAN BRAIN


Comparisons between calculating machines and brains are usually made analytically, at the elementary scale of the electronic tube and the neurone. Comparing these two organs as a whole is certainly more adventurous but perhaps equally useful.

There exist in fact numerous analogies between a calculating machine and the cerebral regions called "associative", the "thinking" regions of the brain:
- both function only when solicited through their adequate pole, outside of which any stimulation is ineffective.
- When one solicits these two organs through their adequate pole, the information can on the other hand be applied at any stage of the input circuit. Thus one can say without ambiguity that calculating machines, like associative regions, are subject to hallucinations.
- In calculating machines as in associative regions, "switching" errors can occur in the circuits which participate in the transformation of information. In both cases there result errors or illusions of perception or judgment.

Beside these analogies there exist however important differences between calculating machines and the brain. One of the principal ones resides in the quasi-infallibility of the former while the latter function with an important and continual "fringe" of errors. It is probably the sterile logic of the former and the fertile error of the latter which constitute the principal difference between the inert machine and the brain which adapts and evolves.

## Notes
- [Translation note: Gastaut was a prominent French neurophysiologist. His comparison of machine "hallucinations" to brain phenomena is striking - he uses this term to describe when input at an intermediate stage of a circuit causes a response as if full external input had occurred. The idea of "fertile error" as distinguishing brains from machines anticipates later discussions of creativity and machine learning.]
