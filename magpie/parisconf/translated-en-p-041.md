in part because, in some cases, one can find simple and useful methods for overcoming the difficulties due to rounding by means of very simple considerations. Among the phenomena, quasi-teratological, there are among others, striking examples opposing those more or less intuitive ideas that no useful information can be obtained from non-significant digits, and that increasing the precision on one part of the calculations does not alter the result. We cite in this regard an example where the correct answer comes uniquely from non-significant digits, as well as another where a differential equation is solved numerically with excellent precision which could still be increased by taking more digits at all stages of the calculation, but which disappears completely if, for example, one takes more digits for the value of the function alone (and not for that of the derivatives, or vice versa). In these two examples, there remain several degrees of freedom, such that they can still be considered as pertaining to systematic calculations.
