5. Activation of preservation systems by the sum of
   coincidences
6. Memorization with a slight diminution over time of the
   information that a certain number of coincidences has been observed
7. Combination of memorized information with a new neutral signal
   to form a new response.

The electronic circuits performing these operations are very simple; the behaviour of the models, thus equipped, follows very closely that of simple animals, in a laboratory studying conditioned reflexes. Neuroses appear and diminish slowly with rest, more rapidly with a shock, permanently when the learning circuit is removed altogether. The necessity of having three distinct time parameters for this apparently simple function is worthy of note.

In this model the memorization process is due to a weakly damped oscillation; if it is not maintained, it falls below the threshold in a few minutes. After each maintenance operation, it is re-established at its original level. After a certain number of these operations, it can become permanent.

Experiments, continuing at the present time, suggest that the structures and mechanisms responsible for these seven operations in animal brains can be identified and isolated. The first four operations are essential components of the statistical estimation of the external environment; the last three constitute memory and recall.

## Notes

- [Translation note: Walter's description of machine "neuroses" - pathological oscillatory states that could be "cured" by rest, shock, or removal of the learning circuit - demonstrated that psychological concepts could have mechanical analogues. His analysis of conditioned reflex learning into seven primitive operations anticipates later computational theories of learning. The observation that three distinct time parameters are needed for learning foreshadows work on temporal aspects of neural computation.]
