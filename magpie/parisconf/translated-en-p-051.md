Gonzalo TORRES-QUEVEDO


THE AUTOMATION WORKS OF THE SPANISH SCHOOL


The most important works on automation in Spain are, I believe, those of my father, Leonardo TORRES-QUEVEDO. It even seems to me that his place in the world is in the very first rank among the precursors of automation, today so widespread. There are certainly scientists who, before him, studied these problems and others who have achieved in our days more complete results from certain points of view. But it is indeed my father who, first, established, fifty years ago, some general principles on calculating machines, and, thirty-six years ago, in a more complete and broader manner, on automatics and the theoretical possibility of constructing automata. At the same time he was constructing various very interesting devices, some as applications or demonstrations of his theories, while others were more or less useful and practical realizations.

Before giving a brief summary of them, I must say a few words about his life. Leonardo TORRES-QUEVEDO was born in 1852 and died in 1936. He obtained the title of Engineer of Bridges and Roads in Spain, but he barely practiced this profession and devoted himself to his scientific and mechanical works. He obtained much success in Spain, France and Portugal. He was always a great friend of France, and had great French friends. May I be permitted to mention here two well-known scientists, M. Gabriel KOENIGS and M. Maurice d'OCAGNE. The first helped him by placing at his disposal his laboratory at the Sorbonne (although the great majority of the devices had been constructed in Spain) and the second published numerous articles on his works. Among other titles of honour, he was president of the Academy of Sciences in Madrid, corresponding member at first, then later associate of that of Paris, doctor honoris causa of the universities of the Sorbonne and Coimbra and he was in possession of several grand Spanish and Portuguese crosses; the French government accorded him the cross of Commander of the Legion of Honour.

## Notes
- [Translation note: Leonardo Torres-Quevedo (1852-1936) was a remarkable Spanish engineer and inventor who built chess-playing automata and pioneered remote control (telekine). His 1914 essay "Essays on Automatics" proved that any finite procedure could be mechanized - anticipating aspects of computability theory. His son Gonzalo presents this historical account to the conference.]
