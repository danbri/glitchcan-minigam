MATHEMATICAL MACHINES IN SWEDEN

At the beginning of 1946, the first reports of the large
arithmetic machines constructed during the war in the United States
were received in Sweden. These machines aroused great interest
and their importance for our country was immediately grasped.
Already a year later, in 1947, the Swedish diet voted a tax of
2,000,000 Swedish crowns (approximately 130,000,000 francs)
for the purpose of acquiring one or more modern arithmetic
machines. In April 1950 the relay machine BARK, constructed
by a group under the direction of C. Palm, was inaugurated in
Stockholm. This machine currently contains more than 5,000
telephone relays of the standard Swedish government type. Instructions
are given to the machine by making connections on a switchboard.
BARK is constructed for 24 binary digits and employs a floating
binary point. An addition is done in 120 milliseconds, a
multiplication in 160 milliseconds. The solution of the problem
is printed in decimal numbers by teletype apparatus.

Under the leadership of the Swedish Directorate for Mathematical
Machines (Matematikmaskinnamnden), the same group has undertaken
the construction of an electronic machine. This machine will be
of the parallel type with 40 binary digits and a fixed binary
point. An addition will be done in approximately 0.04 milliseconds,
a multiplication in 0.27 milliseconds. The machine will probably
be equipped with a fast electronic memory of the Williams type and
a magnetic memory.

During recent years, analog machines have also attracted great
interest in our country.

## Notes
- BARK = Binar Automatisk RelaKalkylator (Binary Automatic Relay Calculator), completed 1950
- C. Palm was a key figure in Swedish computing
- 5,000 telephone relays shows the scale of relay-based machines
- The contrast in speed: relay (120ms addition) vs. planned electronic (0.04ms) is 3000x faster
- Matematikmaskinnamnden (Mathematical Machines Committee) coordinated Swedish computing development
- The electronic machine mentioned became BESK (1953), one of the fastest computers of its time
- The Williams tube memory was widely adopted after Manchester's success
- [Translation note: "diete suedoise" = Swedish Riksdag (parliament); the government investment shows early recognition of computing's importance]
