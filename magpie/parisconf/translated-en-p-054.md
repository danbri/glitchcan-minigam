To construct the chess player, making checkmate with a rook and a king, against a king, presented at the Colloquium, my father first established precise rules which would necessarily lead to checkmate. Then he made the project of the device which would blindly follow these rules. It would be out of place in this summary to attempt to explain the rules and details of the construction of the machine. I shall only say that it is an electro-mechanical machine; that the positions of the 3 pieces, that is to say of the king and the rook of the device, and of the king of its adversary are recorded at every moment in the machine both in horizontal and vertical position and that the move it plays depends on the recorded positions. I must point out that the rules, established by my father and which the device faithfully follows, are not those which lead to winning the game with the smallest number of moves; he did not concern himself with this; he only wanted to demonstrate the possibility that the chess player has of acting apparently, not with the discernment of a strong player but with that of a mediocre player. If the adversary of the machine makes an incorrect move, it warns him by lighting a lamp; at the third incorrect move it no longer wishes to play, one must begin a new game. When the device gives check to the king it announces it by means of a gramophone. Sometimes it forgets to play correctly in accordance with the rules, it gropes, in the end it recovers its memory.


The telekine.- The fundamental principle for the various manoeuvres executed by the servo-motors is to close an electrical circuit for each of the different manoeuvres. The means of action consists in producing a certain number of Hertzian waves, the manoeuvre which takes place depending solely on the number of these waves which have been produced. At each Hertzian wave that the device receives, the armature of an electromagnet acts on a pawl which advances by one step a ratchet wheel to which is joined a commutator which makes contact successively on several electrical studs. It is necessary to prevent the manoeuvres corresponding to intermediate studs from taking place. This is obtained by means of a system called delayed contact. With this device one can control several positions of the rudder; two different speeds forward, two others in reverse, and a state of rest for the propeller. Independently of these manoeuvres one can salute with a flag rising and descending on a mast. If too long a time elapses without the device receiving any signal, the rudder maintains its position and the propeller stops. The same happens if one sends an excessively long signal or if the device suffers an equivalent breakdown.
