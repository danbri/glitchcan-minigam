## Type

## Language

## Translation

F. M. COLEBROOK

THE HIGH-SPEED AUTOMATIC ELECTRONIC ARITHMETIC CALCULATOR

of the NATIONAL PHYSICAL LABORATORY (A.C.E.)

The "Electronics Section" of the National Physical Laboratory,
in collaboration with the Mathematics Division and a small team
seconded from the English Electric Company, is in the process of
designing and constructing a high-speed automatic electronic
arithmetic calculator with large memory capacity - probably
between 10^5 and 10^6 binary digits.

As a preliminary work, the section has just completed a small-scale
Pilot Model, with a memory capacity of 8,000 binary digits,
for the moment.

It is a "serial" type machine, using binary numbers with a pulse
frequency of one million cycles per second, and mercury tube
memory. Input and output are accomplished by means of punched cards
and a specially adapted Hollerith machine. It has approximately
800 electronic tubes distributed over about 40 chassis supports,
and consumes power on the order of 5 kW. It is relatively small,
occupying a floor area of about 5.5 m². It is, nevertheless,
a fully functioning calculator, and has already been used for
an ordered sequence of calculations.

The intention is to increase the memory capacity to about 200,000
binary digits by the addition of an auxiliary (magnetic) memory;
after which the machine will be used for calculation over a
relatively long period - on the order of six months - in order
to acquire the operational experience required to complete the plan
for the A.C.E. itself.

A distinctive feature of the machine is the way it is designed
and used to make the best possible use of the high speed of numbers
(or pulses). In a few words, this result is ensured by spacing
the instructions in the corresponding memories in such a way that,
when an instruction is completely executed, the next instruction
is immediately available. This result is achieved by certain
particular features of the theoretical design and by the corresponding
method of establishing the programme.

## Notes
- A.C.E. = Automatic Computing Engine, designed by Alan Turing at NPL
- The Pilot ACE described here ran its first program in May 1950
- 8,000 binary digits = 8 Kbits = 1 KB of memory
- Mercury delay line memory was a key technology of the era
- 1 MHz pulse rate and 5.5 m^2 footprint were impressive specifications
- The "optimal instruction spacing" technique anticipated modern pipeline optimization
- English Electric later commercialized the design as DEUCE
- [Translation note: "tubes de mercure" = mercury (delay line) tubes, not CRTs; this was acoustic memory using mercury as the transmission medium]
