## Translation Notes

[Original in English] - This abstract was written in English by Louis Couffignal.

[Translation note: "α rhythm" - Alpha rhythm, brain waves in the 8-12 Hz frequency range, discovered by Hans Berger in the 1920s. Couffignal proposes this as the brain's internal clock, analogous to a computer's clock signal - a remarkably modern insight.]

[Translation note: The comparison between "ideal logic performed with computing machines" and "real logic" in human reasoning anticipates the later distinction in cognitive science between normative models of reasoning and descriptive models of actual human cognition.]

[Translation note: Couffignal's optimistic conclusion about increasing "social strength of intelligence" and "intellectual potential" through understanding brain-machine analogies reflects the utopian hopes of early cybernetics - that understanding thinking machines would help humanity think better.]

[Translation note: The framing of the brain as "a machine where thoughts are elaborated" represents the computational theory of mind that would later become dominant in cognitive science, though Couffignal's version retains distinctly French philosophical overtones.]

[Original typos/gallicisms: "rythm" → "rhythm"; "indirecte" → "indirect"; "comparizon" → "comparison"; "enlargment" → "enlargement"; "encreasement" → "increase" - corrected in translation]
