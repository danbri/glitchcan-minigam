A. M. UTTLEY


RECONSTITUTION OF APPARENTLY LOGICAL OPERATIONS

BY MEANS OF CALCULATING MACHINES


The characteristic of a calculating machine is the processing of information, and its arrangement and encoding in a different form. The word "information" is taken here in the sense understood by Claude SHANNON and others, its unit being the binary digit (or bit). A calculating machine stores, transfers, and compares digits. The first two operations, although perhaps difficult to perform, are simple operations; they were first implemented by non-specifically human means when men inscribed durable symbols on paper or stone. The new step is that of automatic comparison.

In calculation, the basic comparison is that of addition. In formal logic, the basic comparison is "and", that is to say, simultaneous existence. All possible forms of comparison can be performed by means of the basic relation "and", together with the idea "not", that is to say, "the opposite of".

Although logical and mathematical speculations do not increase information, they can give it a form more acceptable to the human brain. The translation of foreign languages and the reduction of a very large number of measurements to a simple and general law are examples of this. It is the number of comparisons considered that measures the complexity of the mental process employed.
