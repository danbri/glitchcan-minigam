## Title
THE COMPUTING MACHINE AND FORM (GESTALT)

## Content

One of the leading questions of Cybernetics was as to whether machines could recognize form, or that generalization of form which is known by the German psychologists as "Gestalt"; and in fact, that seemed to be possible.

Proceeding this ideas, Dr McCulloch was trying to make a machine to enable the blind to read by hearing and its structure presented a remarkable analogy with the visual cortex of the human brain. Likewise, an apparatus was built for the totally deaf, (Vocoder), with a vibrator in contact with the ends of the fingers. It was found that this device would give patterns which were remarkably similar mathematically for the same words spoken by different voices, but which were not similar for different words spoken by the same voice.

The mathematician Stephan Bergmann, working on functions of several complex variables, represented in four (or more)-dimensional space, showed also that a machine can have the "perception" of the topological properties of this space which are not in our nervous system. So, Bergmann's ideas suggest that the Gestalt itself may be identified by mathematical means.

Thus it can be thought that a machine can be made to furnish us with the perception of sorts of Gestalt which are not in our nervous system.

[Translation note: "Gestalt" - German psychological term for form/pattern recognition that cannot be reduced to component parts. Wiener's discussion of machines recognizing Gestalt anticipates pattern recognition and computer vision research by decades.]

[Translation note: "Vocoder" - Voice encoder, a technology developed at Bell Labs in the 1930s. Wiener references its application for the deaf, demonstrating the cross-modal sensory substitution research of this era.]

[Translation note: Warren McCulloch's work on neural networks and sensory systems directly influenced early AI research. His collaboration with Walter Pitts on logical calculus of neural activity (1943) was foundational to cybernetics.]

[Translation note: Stephan Bergmann was a mathematician known for work in complex analysis. Wiener's reference to machine perception of higher-dimensional topology anticipates computational topology and machine learning on manifolds.]
