## Translation

Pierre GERMAIN

RESEARCH IN PROGRESS AT THE UNIVERSITY OF BRUSSELS

Exposition of the goals pursued and succinct description of
some research in progress at the Institute of Applied Physics
of the University of Brussels.

Detailed description of a small machine permitting:

a) the calculation of the integral of the product of two
functions, namely       [integral from 0 to infinity] f(x) g(x) dx       [photoelectrical]

b) plotting on photographic paper the representative
points of a curve, the ordinates of the points being the
results provided in binary form by a large machine.

This small machine, largely electronic, is currently
in the process of completion. Its possible use as an
accessory to a large mathematical machine will be more
particularly presented.

## Notes
- Pierre Germain was from the University of Brussels's Institute of Applied Physics
- The device described is an analog/hybrid integrator using photoelectric techniques
- Output to photographic paper was common before CRT displays became standard
- The concept of peripheral devices as "accessories" to main computers was emerging
- The integral calculation suggests analog methods for continuous functions
- [Translation note: The handwritten "photoelectrical" annotation indicates the machine used photoelectric cells for the integration, a form of optical analog computing]
