## Content

W. Ross ASHBY


THE HOMEOSTAT


The principles of calculating machines are probably not those of the living brain, for these two mechanisms have very different ends. The aim of the calculating machine is to obey orders, given arbitrarily; that of the brain is to maintain the essential physiological variables within limits - the "homeostases" of CANNON - for such maintenance is necessary to life. For the philosopher, the brain may well be an organ of reflection, but for the biologist the brain is, like every other organ of the body, a means of survival. Thus, the calculating machine and the living brain serve very different ends, and we may expect that their basic constructions will also be different.

When the organism enters into interaction with its surroundings, each element affects the other; thus, the two sets form a feedback system. If the "disposable constants" of the brain, or parameters, have suitable values, they all act homeostatically; the essential variables are maintained within their normal limits, and the behaviour of the organism is "adapted" to its environment. The problem of the brain is: how to find the suitable values of its parameters when there is no supra-mechanical direction, so that the brain itself can automatically find the suitable values.

The values can be automatically found by any system if it has a second-order feedback, that is to say a feedback which controls and corrects the primary feedbacks; for, if the parameters enter into interaction with the essential variables in such a way that, the latter leaving their own limits, the parameters pass from their erroneous values to a new set

## Notes
- [Translation note: W. Ross Ashby (1903-1972) was a British psychiatrist and pioneer of cybernetics. His Homeostat (1948) was a famous electromechanical device that demonstrated adaptive, self-organizing behavior through feedback mechanisms. CANNON refers to Walter Bradford Cannon who introduced the concept of "homeostasis" in 1926. Ashby's distinction between calculating machines (which follow orders) and brains (which maintain homeostasis for survival) remains insightful for understanding the limits of symbolic AI approaches.]
