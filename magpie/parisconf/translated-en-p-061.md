## Content

Norbert WIENER


CALCULATING MACHINES AND FORM (GESTALT)


One of the first questions of Cybernetics was whether a machine was capable of recognizing form, or even that generalization of form which German psychologists know under the name of "Gestalt"; and this did indeed appear to be possible.

In pursuit of these ideas, Doctor McCulloch endeavored to construct a machine enabling the blind to "read" by audition, and whose structure presented a remarkable analogy with the visual cortex of the human brain. Similarly, an apparatus (Vocoder) was constructed for the totally deaf, incorporating a vibrator in contact with the fingertips. It was found that the apparatus formed very similar mathematical patterns for the same word pronounced by different voices, and different patterns for words pronounced by the same voice.

The mathematician Stephan Bergmann, studying functions of several complex variables representable in four-dimensional space or more, also showed that a machine could have the "perception" of the topological properties of that space, which is impossible for our nervous system. Bergmann's ideas thus suggest that Gestalt itself can be identified by mathematical means.

One can thus conceive that machines could be constructed capable of rendering perceptible forms currently inaccessible to the human brain. This use of calculating machines appears more interesting than applications to numerical calculations, even those currently unrealizable by other means.
