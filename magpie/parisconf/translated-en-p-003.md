MONDAY 8 JANUARY at 3:00 p.m., at the Institut Blaise Pascal.

Visit of the laboratories, under the direction of MM. J. Peres, L. Couffignal, L. Malavard.
Presentation of the pilot machine of the Institut Blaise Pascal (I.B.P.): L. Couffignal, P. Angelle.

TUESDAY 9 JANUARY at 9:00 a.m.
                              at the National Centre for Pedagogical Documentation.

Research in progress at the University of Brussels: M. P. Germain.
Short-cut multiplication in a parallel-Decimal Automatic Calculating Machine
      (R.A.E.S.C.C.): E. J. Petherick. [Original title in English]
Summary of computer research at Manchester University: F. C. Williams. [Original title in English]
Discussion on arithmetic machines.

TUESDAY 9 JANUARY at 2:15 p.m.
                              at the National Centre for Pedagogical Documentation.

The tripole — its use for the instantaneous resolution of certain
      trigonometric problems: A. G. Del Valle.
Mathematical machines in Sweden: S. Ekelof.
Rheoelectric analogy methods and machines: L. Malavard.
The electronic simulator for guided missile motion: Cdt Coufleau.
Electronic differential analyser (S.E.A.): F. H. Raymond.
      a) General theory;
      b) Descriptive film (in English).
Discussion on analog machines.
General discussion on the work of the 1st Section.

## Notes
- R.A.E.S.C.C. = Royal Aircraft Establishment Special Computing Circuit
- S.E.A. = Societe d'Electronique et d'Automatisme (Electronics and Automation Company)
- "machines arithmetiques" = arithmetic machines (i.e., digital calculating machines, as opposed to analog)
- "machines analogiques" = analog machines (computing by physical analogy rather than discrete calculation)
- [Translation note: "rheoelectric" refers to electrical resistance-based analog computing methods]
