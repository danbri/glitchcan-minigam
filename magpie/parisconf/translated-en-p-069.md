It is therefore much more than a simple calculating machine, however powerful it may be.

The second difference between the brain and a calculating machine is a difference of category. Arithmetic machines - or, insofar as logic has been reduced to arithmetic - "logical" machines deal with affirmations and their negations. Experimental neurophysiology began some 500 years before Christ, when ALCMAEON of CROTON, the first neurosurgeon, noted that our sense organs establish "contraries" and transmit them to our brain which reunites them in a synthesis in accord with that harmony which we call health. Each pair of contraries defines a qualitative axis in the disorder of the lived world, a disorder from which emerge bundles of "qualities" that will populate our universe with appearances of substances; their positions and their displacements permit us to construct our common universe of physics and our superstitious belief in its causality. Allow me to replace causality where it must be, psychologically speaking, with time and space, as a form of sensation. We thus add to the contraries of ALCMAEON, cause and effect. To survive, living beings must perceive the world according to these denominations. Calculating machines, for their part, need not.

Thirdly, calculation was originally a means for bringing to a common measure facts of every kind; one then placed, for calculation, pebbles in pots. Calculating machines substitute for man's hand in the placement and displacement of pebbles from one pot to another according to the ancient rules of Arithmetic. Following HUME's conception of equal numbers, we have come to define a number as the class of all classes that can be put into one-to-one correspondence with any class of realities. Manual operations with pebbles replace conceptual operations with numbers so conceived, that if we descended to HADES to find spiritual ancestors for this conception, ABELARD of PARIS would not be the least among them.

WILLIAM of OCKHAM, who advised avoiding multiplying entities without necessity, insisted on the difference between thinking in "natural" terms (which we share with other animals) and thinking in "conventional" terms from which man alone benefits. Any number greater than six is in this sense conventional and not natural, and the use of pebbles is clearly a "convention". From this it follows that the "natural" terms of a calculating machine are the conventional terms of man. These machines thus hold the role held by our hands in arithmetic operations; they do not replace our brain.

## Translation Notes

- "calcul" derives from Latin "calculus" meaning pebble - McCulloch is making an etymological point
- ALCMAEON of Croton (fl. 5th century BCE) - Greek philosopher and medical theorist, often considered the first neuroscientist
- [Translation note: McCulloch's invocation of medieval philosophers (Abelard, Ockham) reflects his deep philosophical education and his view that these questions have ancient roots]
- The distinction between "natural" and "conventional" thinking anticipates later debates about core cognition and cultural cognition
