## Translation from French

- 7 -

The vestibular apparatus, which detects accelerations and rotations, communicates with other parts of the brain through relay systems; the spontaneous activity in these circuits undergoes frequency modulation by the recorded accelerations. The same thing seems to exist for the cerebellum and its efferent nuclei, which derive phylogenetically from vestibular structures: these nuclei served to coordinate the acceleration of head movements with the movements and acceleration undergone by the body and limbs, and to subordinate these to commands from other cerebral structures. The essential role of this system is to calculate the efferences that will bring to rest all parts of our body that had been set in motion. In this process, if the rhythm of spontaneous discharge of the neurons of the system was first accelerated, it will be slowed; conversely, if it was slowed, it will subsequently be accelerated. The discharge frequencies of these neurons seem to overshoot their goal in the direction opposite to that in which they were originally pushed.

A complex example of this type of mechanism is found in nystagmus, a sort of oscillating movement whose displacement curve is of the "sawtooth" type: this aspect reveals a typical "reverberation" activity in the neurons; this activity, as long as it persists, will attribute a rotation to the external world, a rotation that really existed only at the phase of triggering the phenomenon. It goes without saying that all these views are predictions concerning possible mechanisms and are given as examples of application of the hypotheses stated above and therefore, like all hypotheses, may prove false.

Another variety of encoding depends on the counting of impulses. Dr. DOTY has recently shown that a determined number of successive impulses arriving via the laryngeal nerve triggers swallowing. The precision of this counting process is limited by the fact that the signals to be counted must neither arrive too frequently, at the risk of some being omitted, nor too dispersed, at the risk of weakening through the circuits. Since the counters in this type of reflex arc are few and relatively simple, it may perhaps be possible to know how this counting is achieved.

## Translation Notes

- "appareil vestibulaire" - vestibular apparatus, the inner ear system responsible for balance and spatial orientation
- "nystagmus" - nystagmus, involuntary rhythmic eye movements
- Dr. DOTY - Robert Doty (1920-2011), American neurophysiologist known for work on the neural basis of swallowing and other reflexes
- [Translation note: The "reverberation" activity McCulloch describes relates to his theory of reverberating circuits as a basis for short-term memory, developed with Lorente de No]
- [Translation note: The discussion of neural "counting" mechanisms anticipates later research on temporal processing and numerosity in neuroscience]
