## Translation

A. D. BOOTH

A MAGNETIC AUTOMATIC CALCULATING MACHINE

A. D. BOOTH

The two machines that have been constructed under the auspices
of Birkbeck College, University of London, will be described.

The first, a prototype called S.E.C., employs approximately
250 tubes for a memory of 256 words of 6 decimals each, but
does not possess a multiplier.

The final model A.P.E.X.C. employs approximately 275 tubes
and possesses fully automatic multiplication as well as
other capabilities linked to a memory capacity of 512
words of 9 decimals each. Both machines use a magnetic
drum memory providing high-speed recording.

## Notes
- A.D. Booth was a British computer pioneer at Birkbeck College, London
- S.E.C. = Simple Electronic Computer (1948)
- A.P.E.X.C. = All Purpose Electronic X-ray Computer (1952)
- "Tubes" refers to vacuum tubes (thermionic valves), the primary logic elements before transistors
- Magnetic drum memory was a key early storage technology
- 256-512 words was typical memory capacity for early machines
- [Translation note: "6 decimales" / "9 decimales" refers to decimal digit precision per word, reflecting the decimal (not binary) orientation of some early machines]
