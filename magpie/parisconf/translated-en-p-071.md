Claude SHANNON had, without our knowledge, applied the propositional calculus to circuits composed of open or closed relays; Walter PITTS and I have developed a mode of calculation applying to these nervous signals and deriving from the propositional calculus; the symbol corresponding to each neuron is assigned an index characterizing the moment at which a signal affected that neuron. The central nervous system will be considered as a network of such neurons; on the input side, a signal in a sensory neuron depends on a signal imposed on the peripheral receptor that corresponds to it. These two signals are separated by a time equal to the relay passage time. The relation between neurons working thus is obviously transitive, and any event whatsoever in the central nervous system can always be related to antecedent signals that existed at the network's input. The existence of such a network of neurons, carrying signals that can be logical sums or products, with and without negation, of the signals furnished to it at the input; the possibility of repetitive discharges in a neuron, unless it is inhibited, constitute more than sufficient properties for it to be able to develop complex functions from simple propositions. Thus an appropriate network can replace, with a simple signal in a particular neuron, a defined configuration of input signals, linked by temporal and spatial relations. Spatial configurations can be transformed into temporal configurations and vice versa. In the transport of this information, many neurons can be replaced simply by delays due to relay times.

It is possible for us to introduce the logical operators "some" and "all" by using circuits formed along which trains of signals that have preserved the configuration they had at the input circulate while constantly regenerating; indeed, an input signal that appeared at a defined instant is reproduced at all subsequent instants. Because of this, the precise moment when it was introduced into the network no longer matters, and we can say "there was a time when such and such an event took place."
