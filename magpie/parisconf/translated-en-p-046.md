G. R. BOULANGER


MECHANICAL CALCULATION AND THE ART OF THE ENGINEER


1. The importance of numerical calculation for the Engineer is well known. One notes however, in the latter, an almost total ignorance of the current possibilities of calculating machines (except in the field of aeronautics; see paragraph 3). This paradoxical situation is due, principally, to the fact that mechanical calculation was born and develops "outside" the Art of the civil engineer. The development is rapid, and utilization does not follow.

It becomes urgent, facing this deficiency, to establish for the engineer an account of new possibilities (machines), to ask him to draw up an inventory of his needs (applications), and to put at his disposal tools ready for use (methods).

The present communication has as its object to take stock regarding these fundamental aspects of the mechanization of scientific and technical calculation. We shall draw from it directives valid for the immediate future.

2. Faced with the diversity of mechanical calculation devices currently usable (the expression "mechanical calculation" being taken in the extended sense of "automatic calculation"), it is convenient to make the distinction between (1) arithmetic machines and analog (or algebraic) machines, (2) laboratory machines and commercial machines, (3) large and small machines. From the point of view of applications to the Art of the Engineer, one can then show that interest is essentially concentrated, for the moment, on three fundamental combinations, namely:

a) Large arithmetic laboratory machines;
b) Small commercial arithmetic machines;
c) Commercial analog machines of various sizes.
