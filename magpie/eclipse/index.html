Never look at the Sun

The Sun can hurt your eyes for ever. It does not hurt at the time. That is why it is dangerous.

  • Do not look with your eyes.
  • Do not look through sunglasses.
  • Do not look through a camera, binoculars or a telescope.
  • Only look through real eclipse glasses. A grown-up must check them first.

The times where you are

    Which way?

    Look west

    The Sun is low down.

    A phone compass can be wrong near cars and buildings. The word above is always right.

    Find a low view

    The Sun is close to the rooftops. Houses, walls and trees will hide it. Find a park, a hill, a bridge, or a beach that looks out to the west.

    How high is it?

    Hold your arm out straight. Make a fist. One fist is about ten degrees.

    Point your face, not your eyes. Put your eclipse glasses on before you look up.

    The one big rule

    Never look straight at the Sun.

    Your eyes have no pain sensor at the back. The Sun can burn the back of your eye and you will feel nothing until later.

    Eclipse glasses

    • They must say ISO 12312-2 on them.
    • A grown-up must look at them first.
    • Hold them up to a lamp. You must see almost nothing.
    • If you see a scratch or a hole, do not use them. Throw them away.
    • Put them on before you look up. Take them off after you look down.
    • Never use them with binoculars, a telescope or a camera. The heat will burn straight through.

    The safest way: make a pinhole

    You look down, not up. The Sun makes its own little picture on the ground.

    1. Take a piece of card.
    2. Make one small hole in it with a pencil.
    3. Stand with your back to the Sun.
    4. Hold the card up and let the light go through the hole on to the path.
    5. Look at the little Sun on the ground. It is upside down.

    Move the card away from the ground to make the picture bigger and fainter.

    Two more tricks

    A kitchen colander. Every hole makes a little Sun. You get a whole shower of them.

    A tree. The gaps between the leaves work the same way. The ground under a tree fills up with little crescent Suns. This is the best thing to look for, and most people walk over it and never notice.

    Why does this happen?

    1. The Moon goes around the Earth. It takes about one month.
    2. Sometimes the Moon goes right in front of the Sun.
    3. The Moon then makes a shadow. The shadow falls on the Earth.
    4. If you stand in the shadow, you see the Sun go dark.

    The best trick in space

    The Sun is 400 times bigger than the Moon. But the Sun is also 400 times farther away.

    So they look the same size in the sky. That is why the Moon can cover the Sun so well. No other planet we know gets this lucky.

    Why is it not black here?

    You are near the edge of the shadow, not in the middle. The middle of the shadow crosses Greenland, Iceland and the north of Spain today. There the Sun goes black for a minute or two.

    Where you are, the Moon takes a very big bite instead. It gets strange and silvery, but it does not get dark like night.

      Feel it too

      Close your eyes for ten seconds near the biggest bite. Then open them. The light looks wrong, like a photograph with the colour turned down.

      Put your hand out. The air gets cooler. In a big eclipse it can drop by a few degrees.

      Listen. Birds often stop singing. Some go to bed.
