splatweb — presence, not pixels

Instead of streaming video of your room and face, stream a 32-byte pose packet at 30 Hz plus voice, and let viewers reconstruct you inside a cached, CDN-served Gaussian-splat "room avatar". ~40 kbit/s of live traffic instead of ~1,500 — and fan-out to thousands without a video transcode in sight. Design doc: DESIGN.md (original draft: raw.txt) · borrowing catalogue (capture stacks & open avatars, licences checked).

DEMO 1

The 32-byte packet

Live hex view of the wire format. Drive a face, watch it encode, decode it into a second face, read the quantization error and the bandwidth-vs-video meter. Includes the Tier-3 Canvas-2D renderer.
DEMO 2

The room avatar

A ~3,000-splat procedural room rendered by a dependency-free WebGL2 Gaussian-splat rasterizer (Tier 1). Orbit, zoom, and try the in-shader stylise slider.
DEMO 3

The stage — full pipeline

Pose → packet → simulated lossy network (latency / jitter / loss sliders) → jitter buffer with interpolation and capped dead-reckoning → splat room + splat avatar. Real capture: 🎤 MIC (speech energy → jaw) and 📷 CAM (MediaPipe face tracking → pose + expressions). Crank the loss and watch presence survive.
DEMO 4

Borrowed models, splat-ified

Vendored open models (Lee Perry-Smith head scan CC BY, 100Avatars CC BY) converted to Gaussian splats in the browser at load time — triangles area-sampled, textures read per point, rendered by the same splat renderer.

Honesty box: capture and network are simulated in-page; the room is procedural, not a scan; no XMPP yet. What is real: the wire format, the receiver model, and the splat renderer. See DESIGN.md §6.
