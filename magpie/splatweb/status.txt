What changed

- The rigged VRM roster is now the stage avatar. New module lib/rigged-splats.js samples a VRM once, keeps joints, weights, normals and the VRM mouth and blink morphs, and skins all splats on the CPU each frame. Cost is 3 to 6 ms for 18,000 splats at 60 fps with WebGPU.
- Stored animations and IK. New module lib/skeleton-anim.js has clips for idle, walk, wave, nod, shrug, dance, talk and sit, pose blending, two-bone arm IK against the rig's own bone lengths, and a composer that layers the packet over the clip. The head splits 35 percent neck and 65 percent head. The mouth and blinks drive the VRM presets.
- Walking. The sender wanders in auto mode. The packet position field moves the avatar. The viewer turns position deltas into a stride and a heading. No new bytes on the wire.
- The cartoony guest is gone. All eleven VRMs are in the avatar carousel. The procedural presets and Face Cap stay as options. Kyle is the default.
- The stats chip is hideable. The small ⓘ chip at bottom left toggles it. The choice is remembered. Use ?stats=0 to start hidden.
- 13 rooms in lib/rooms.js. Six procedural: studio, library, rooftop, beach pier, theatre, grotto. Seven from dbdb stamps at varied scales: conservatory, ghost garden, ghost town, hedge maze, glasshouse, forest path, winter grove, garage yard. The library has a mezzanine, a staircase, tables and an armchair. Critters hop between the levels there, and in the theatre tiers, the grotto ledges and the rooftop.
- Arms. Capture now uses the full MediaPipe pose model, sends elbows and torso in a 52-byte packet v2, filters landmarks with a OneEuro filter, and the viewer chases each wrist with a critically damped spring, a 3 m/s speed cap, and fade-in and fade-out when tracking drops. This is not verified with a live webcam. I tested it with synthetic targets only.
- Rig lab page for experiments. Gestures also have buttons on the stage sender panel.

Why not a different arm library. MediaPipe Pose is the standard in-browser skeleton. The visible problems came from the lite model's poor depth, a guessed elbow, and no filtering. Those three are fixed. A different library gives no better data.

Known limits. No finger or gaze animation. Feet are not planted. The upper-arm twist under IK is the minimal rotation. The Calico Tanks rock stamps and the museum floors are too sparse to make good rooms, so I did not use them.
