foafos alpha1

One shell, four installations. Each is a root manifest — data, not a fork — naming what the root may do, what it boots, and which apps exist at all. Everything below the root is bounded by it, because a child can never hold a capability its parent lacks.

WHAT TO TRY

  • Apps (drawer → ⊞, or Alt+H) lists only what that installation offers. Office shows no games.
  • Running (⧉, or Alt+Tab) shows the app tree. Start a story, let it open a game, and the game appears indented beneath it. The ⏸ and ✕ on a row act on the whole subtree and say how many go with it, and whether closing loses the game's state.
  • Close and reopen a game. Mudslider comes back in the room you left it, with your score and lives. Chess comes back to the same position. GridLuck does not — it predates the SDK, and the switcher says so rather than losing your game quietly.
  • Logger (Apps → Make → 📜) is the event bus, filterable. Filter for refused to watch the capability system say no.
  • Volume (drawer) reports what it cannot reach as well as what it can.
  • On a phone, the d-pad is the shell's, not the game's — one pad, shared, and it retires when a real gamepad appears.

HONEST STATUS

  • Four apps still run with ambient authority (same-origin) because they use localStorage/indexedDB heavily and have not been moved onto the storage broker. The drawer names them. Calendar has been migrated — it used IndexedDB, which an opaque origin refuses, so its store now falls back to the broker.
  • Stories are more privileged than apps, not less: the narrative runtime is the host page, so a story can launch, navigate and restyle. Its capability list describes rather than constrains, and says so.
  • Closing a game now keeps its place, even across a reload — but only for guests that agreed to the snapshot contract (mudslider, chess). The switcher tells you which games those are before you press ✕. Finish a game and the save clears, so you can start again.
  • Tellyclub's preferences do not persist here — it predates the SDK and gets an opaque origin, which it survives only because its author wrote defensive storage code.
