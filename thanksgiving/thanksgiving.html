    For grown-ups: how this page was made

    Every event here comes from a written record or from Wampanoag accounts of their own history. Where historians are not sure, the page says so. Some things we do not know: the exact day of the 1621 feast, whether Massasoit was invited or arrived for another reason, which sickness emptied Patuxet, and the exact path the Mayflower took across the ocean (the line on the map is a drawing, not a log).

    Two calendars. In 1620 England used the old Julian calendar, which was 10 days behind the calendar we use now. William Bradford and Edward Winslow wrote their dates in the old calendar, so the page gives both. The English also began the new year on March 25, so Bradford writes "1620" for events from January 1 to March 24, 1621.

    The hard parts. The page tells children, in plain words, about the 1614 kidnapping, the epidemic, the corn and graves the English took on Cape Cod, the war of 1675, and the National Day of Mourning. The Wampanoag Nation is not only in the past: it is here today, with its own governments, schools and language.
