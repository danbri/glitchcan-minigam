❄️ SPLATPORT — locations wished into existence

Prototype: a real Gaussian splat capture (public-domain scan by Keijiro Takahashi, prefix-LOD'd from 1.77M splats) rendered in the browser, with an ink story — the Portkeeper — standing inside the scan on a live, tappable perspective-matched screen. Choices summon snow and bells. Drag to orbit, pinch to approach. ?hq loads the 400k-splat cut.

Best on: Mobile or desktop (WebGL2)

🐤 CANARY WHARF — one small bird, one big skyline

A one-thumb flight game over a stylised Docklands at golden dusk. Hold to climb, release to glide, drag to bank. Light seven rooftop beacons among glass towers with TSL curtain-wall shaders — and read the lamplighters' story in ink, playing on a live interactive screen standing in the 3D world (real inkjs compile; choices grant flight boons). Gaussian-splat-style swirl transitions, paved promenades, a DLR loop, a murmuration, a peregrine. Also playable inside FINK stories via # MINIGAME: canarywharf.

Best on: Mobile (one thumb) — desktop: hold Space, steer with arrows

🐥 Jet Set Ducky — a 3D Maisonette Misadventure

Part Jet Set Willy, part Ant Attack, part Prince of Persia: a scrolling isometric platformer in C64 colours, rendered with Three.js TSL shaders (dithered blocks, banded water, CRT scanlines + barrel distortion). A rotoscope-style human hero — hyper-real against the chunky 8-bit manor, Ducky riding shoulder — must collect all 25 flashing items across ten named rooms and get to bed before midnight. THE WIRELESS plays 50 tagged chip tunes that follow the mood. Rev 2.1 adds Moonfall Parade: a pub with a jostling crowd and a back-slapping barkeep couple, maglev shoppers hailing hover-cars, and a corner-booth terminal you can jack into — a nested ink story inside a real Gaussian splat. ?hero=duck restores the waddling original.

Best on: Mobile (touch stick + jump button) — also keyboard (arrows/WASD + Space, M mutes)

🌍 Simulations & Visualizations

🌒 ECLIPSE WATCH — a kids' guide to the 2026 eclipse

A safe, mobile-first eclipse guide written for young children. Every number is computed on the device from Astronomy Engine (MIT, vendored, no CDN), so the times, the direction and the coverage are right for wherever you say you are — London by default, where the Moon covers 91% of the Sun at 19:13 BST on 12 August 2026.

Five screens: a live sky drawn from the real disc geometry with a scrubber that plays the whole eclipse in 22 seconds; a compass that says which way to face; the safety rules with a draggable pinhole-projector demo; an animated explainer; and a checklist of things to notice that need no looking up. Reads itself aloud for children who cannot yet read, keeps the screen awake, and works with no signal at all.

Best on: Mobile (portrait, one thumb) — works offline once opened
