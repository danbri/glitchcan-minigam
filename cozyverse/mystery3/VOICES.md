# Lock Fourteen: voice sheets (gate 1, for the owner)

One sheet per speaking person. The ink is written against these, and a reviewer checks each line against its
speaker's sheet. Rules for all of them, from the chapter 2 review: a joke comes from what the person wants in the
scene, not from wit; no closing quips; a running joke is used at most twice; a scene ends on what happens.

The narration is second person ("you") and is Ros's attention: what she looks at, in the order she looks at it.
Short sentences. No comment on what things mean. It does not tell the reader what to feel.

---

**Ros Kettering** (the reader)
- Words: the words of her forms, said as normal speech: "the deceased", "next of kin", "for the coroner",
  "sudden and unexplained". Asks short questions and waits.
- Sentences: short. Often a question back instead of an answer.
- Wants: no open files when she retires in March. In each scene, the one fact she came for.
- Never says: "murder", until page 10. Never threatens anyone; she has nothing to threaten with.
- Humour: people take her for the admin, and she lets them, because they say more.

**Clive Amory** (the killer)
- Words: precise and kind. Explains well, with numbers ("a lock this size holds about sixty tonnes").
  Says "we" for the trust.
- Sentences: long, complete, patient; the longest of anyone.
- Wants: the inquest to be short, and the trust to go on. He believes he has saved it.
- Never says: Neville's first name after the death; he says "the chairman". Never raises his voice.
- Tell, when pressed: he explains more, never less.

**Kit Rowe**
- Words: practical, canal words used without explanation ("pound", "cill", "lock landing").
- Sentences: quick and flat, with the important part last.
- Wants: the Bristol job, and nobody at the trust to know until she has it.
- Never says: anything bad about Neville, who was hard on her and taught her everything.

**Joost van Dam**
- Words: good English, slightly formal, Dutch order now and then ("Then we have the paddle left open").
- Wants: this holiday to be over, and not to be blamed for a stranger's death.
- Never says: sorry, until he has to.

**Maaike van Dam**
- Words: direct. Corrects Joost in English so that Ros hears it.
- Wants: to tell the truth from the start; she lies only because Joost asked her to.
- Her photographs are her evidence: she offers her phone before she is asked.

**Annette Strand**
- Words: a teacher's: clear, exact, a little dry. Calls her husband "Neville", never "my husband".
- Sentences: complete; pauses where other people would cry.
- Wants: to know exactly what happened in the last hour, more than she wants anyone punished.
- Never says: that Neville was difficult, although he was. She lets other people say it.

**Pete Garrow**
- Words: few. Times and drinks: "One pint of Hook Norton, half eight, same as every Thursday."
- Wants: no police in the pub on a Saturday.
- Notices cars and who pays.

**Dr Imogen Shah** (phone)
- Words: medical, then the plain version without being asked. Gives facts, not opinions, and says which is which.
- Wants: to be off the phone by the time her train leaves.

**DS Sam Adeyemi** (page 6, short)
- As in Steeple Wyke. Polite; tells Ros there is no crime, and means well. Will be wrong.

**DCI Hester Quaile** (page 10)
- As in Steeple Wyke. Reads the whole file in silence before she says anything. Her hearing aid is on.
