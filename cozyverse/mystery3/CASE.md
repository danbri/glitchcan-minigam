# Lock Fourteen: the case file (gate 1, for the owner)

Status: gate 2, October 2026. Premise A of `PREMISES.md`, chosen by the owner, who answered the three open
questions (at the end). Nothing here is story text: this is the truth the story is built on, and the list of what
the reader can find and where. The text-only playable draft is `lockfourteen.ink`
(https://danbri.github.io/glitchcan-minigam/cozyverse/mystery3/), and this file was brought into line with it.

## The place

The Wyke Arm: a canal branch two miles below Steeple Wyke, which a volunteer trust is bringing back into use. Locks
11 to 16 climb the valley; lock 11 is lowest, by the Navigation Inn. The trust's office is the old lock cottage at
lock 13, with a gravel car park beside it. Locks 13 to 16 are restored. At night the trust chains the paddle gear of
each restored lock to save water; the person who walks the flight last thing does it. Three people have a key to
the chains: Neville Strand, Clive Amory and Kit Rowe.

The lock logic, which page 1 teaches: a boat that goes down through a lock leaves it empty; a boat that goes up
leaves it full. To fill a lock you wind up the top paddles with a windlass, and the water comes from the stretch of
canal above (the pound), which drops as the lock fills.

## The people

- **Ros Kettering**, 58, coroner's officer. The reader ("you").
- **Neville Strand**, 66, the dead man. Chairman of the trust. Retired water engineer. Walked the flight every
  night at ten and chained the locks. Shouted at boaters who left paddles up. Kept every receipt.
- **Annette Strand**, 64, his wife. Retired primary-school head. She wants to know what happened more than she
  wants anyone punished.
- **Clive Amory**, 61, the trust's treasurer. Retired bank manager. Kind, exact, the best explainer on the canal.
  The killer.
- **Kit Rowe**, 24, the trust's only paid lock-keeper. Lives in the lock cottage. Wants a career on the water.
- **Joost and Maaike van Dam**, 34 and 33, from Utrecht, on a hire boat called *Wren*. First canal holiday.
- **Pete Garrow**, 50s, landlord of the Navigation Inn.
- **Dr Imogen Shah**, the pathologist (by phone).
- **DCI Hester Quaile** (Steeple Wyke chapters), on the last page. DS Sam Adeyemi has one short scene on page 6.

## What happened

**Two years ago.** Clive put the trust's £150,000 reserve into a fund that failed. He told nobody. The accounts he
showed the committee carried the reserve at its old value.

**This spring.** The trust won a heritage grant of £180,000 for two pairs of new oak gates, for locks 13 and 14.
Clive placed the order with a small gate-maker, Hollins & Daughter, whose owner, Ted Hollins, was his friend at
school. Hollins supplied reclaimed gates from a rebuilt lock in the north, painted them, and invoiced £180,000 for new
ones. Hollins kept £30,000 and in June paid £150,000 back to the trust as an "anonymous donation", which Clive put in
the reserve account. The reserve was whole again. Clive kept nothing.

Case-board conclusions. The board is page 8 and the hub between visits; thinking there costs no action, but a
wrong join closes that question until the next day. Six cards; each opens a question:

- 2 + (1 or 3) → "Someone filled lock 14 after the last boat" → *who would be at a lock at night?*
- 12 + that card → "He was alive when the lock was filled" → *not an accident* (with 12a, it holds in court).
- 9 + 4 → "The chain was unlocked after 23:13, and only Clive's key was free" → *Clive must say where he was.*
- 8 + (11 or 10) → "Clive went to lock 14 after the call" → *why did he say he went to bed?*
- 13 + (14 or 5) → "The gates are old" → *who signed to say they were new?*
- 14 (reserve) + (14 grant or 13) → "The gate money went round in a circle" → *why steal money and give it back?*

## Statements the reader can disprove

The reader presents a clue against a statement. The right clue opens the next statement; a wrong one costs trust,
and that person answers less from then on.

| person | first statement | the clue that breaks it | the truth behind the lie |
|---|---|---|---|
| Joost | "We did not see him after lock fifteen." | 6 (the photograph at 22:41) | Neville took his windlass; Joost pulled away; they were afraid it would look like a fight |
| Kit | "I was here all night. I heard nothing." | 4 (her key in Neville's coat) or 15 | the Bristol interview, which she does not want the trust to know about |
| Clive (first, page 1) | "He rang me at five to eleven, about the minutes. I told him to ring me in the morning, and I went to bed." | 11 (his car at 23:40) | he went to lock 14 |
| Clive (second, page 9) | "I went down, he wasn't there, I sat in the car, I came home." | 9 (Neville alive at 23:13, at lock 14) | the meeting, the fall, the paddles; he asks to stop |

Each person who lies stops talking after two wrong items (Kit shuts the door; Joost asks for a lawyer; Clive says
"through the trust's solicitor").

## The suspects the evidence partly supports

- **The van Dams.** For: Neville shouted at them twice, they lied, and their windlass was at the scene. Against:
  the camera, the pub, and Neville alive at 23:13.
- **Kit Rowe.** For: a key, a motive (the minutes show Neville proposed ending her post), and a lie about where she
  was. Against: the ticket and the interview letter; she gave her key away.
- **Nobody (an accident).** For: a dark towpath and a man of 66. Against: the low alcohol, the full lock, the chain
  put back on.

## The endings

On page 10 Ros completes her report in three parts:

1. **The conclusion she recommends**: accident, open, or unlawful killing.
2. **How**: fell; was pushed; or fell and was drowned when the lock was filled.
3. **Whom she names to the police, and why**: nobody; the van Dams; Kit; Clive for theft; Clive for the death, to
   hide the lost reserve.

Quaile asks one question about the chain when the report names Clive for the death; the right answer needs the
chain card.

Clive's last speech (owner: "enough to seed ideas for other episodes/chapters but no llm waffle"): how he did it, in
two lines, then two leads for later stories, then one sentence. The leads: Ted Hollins put the parish council's
money into the same failed fund; Hollins has made "new" gates for four other trusts since 1990.

## The moment the plot does not use

On Sunday Annette shows Ros Neville's notebook of lock readings: the water level at each lock, every night, for
eleven years, in pencil. The last entry is Thursday, lock 15. Nobody refers to it again.

## The canary

As in Steeple Wyke, one rare panel shows a small yellow bird once in a reading. The text never mentions it.
